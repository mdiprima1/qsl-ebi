SLIDE 2 — INTUITION
QSL
Compounding

The earliest money works the longest

  • A natural answer: the early starter deposited a third more — $144,000 against $108,000 — so he should finish about a third ahead.
  • The chart says otherwise. At 65 the accounts stand at $890,000 against $400,000 — the early starter finishes with more than twice as much. The deposits alone cannot explain that gap.
  • What explains it: money that stays invested earns money, and those earnings stay invested and earn money of their own.
  • The ten extra years belong to the earliest dollars. A dollar deposited at 25 has forty years to earn, and its earnings have decades to earn too.

SLIDE 3 — DEFINITION
QSL
Compounding

Compound growth

  • Compound growth is growth on growth: an investment grows by a percentage of its current value, so each year’s growth builds on all the growth that came before.
  • One line of arithmetic describes it. Read the formula as a sentence: the starting amount, grown by the rate, once for every year.
  • One step of it: $100,000 at 7.7% becomes $107,700 after one year. The next year’s 7.7% lands on $107,700, not on $100,000.
  • The next slide walks the formula through thirty years.
FV = P × (1 + r)n
P — the starting amount invested
r — the annual return, as a decimal: 7.7% is 0.077
n — the number of years the money stays invested
FV — the ending value after those years

SLIDE 4 — WORKED EXAMPLE
QSL
Compounding

One hundred thousand dollars, thirty years

  • Take $100,000 growing at 7.7% a year — the same 60/40 average — and look at what single years earn.
  • Year one earns $7,700. Year two starts from $107,700, so the same 7.7% earns $8,293.
  • By year ten, the balance entering the year is $194,958 and the year earns $15,012 — nearly double year one.
  • By year thirty, a single year earns $66,183 — more than eight times year one — with nothing added along the way.

SLIDE 5 — CONSIDERATIONS
QSL
Compounding

What the steady rate leaves out

  • No market pays the same rate every year. The 7.7% on these slides is an average over 2005–2026; the real path swings around it, some years far above, some far below. Later units measure those swings.
  • The rate and the years multiply. $500 a month for forty years ends at $365,000 at 2.0%, $1,500,000 at 7.7%, and $2,700,000 at 9.9%. The deposits are identical in every row — the return decides the size of the result.
  • The figures are before costs, taxes and inflation. Each takes its share of the ending value, and the course returns to all three.
  • The return number is the subject of this course. A difference of two percentage points, held for decades, separates outcomes by hundreds of thousands of dollars — which is why the rest of the course is about understanding, measuring, and choosing that return honestly.
