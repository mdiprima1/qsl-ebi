The typical size of a daily move

  • One natural answer: compare the worst single day. In May 2026, TSLA’s worst day was a loss of 4.75%. AAPL’s was a loss of 1.18%.
  • A single day is one event, not a pattern. A stock could have one bad day in an otherwise calm month.
  • The measure we want describes every day in the period: the typical size of a daily move.
  • On a typical day in May 2026, TSLA moved 2.1% up or down. AAPL moved 0.9%. The two stocks differed in risk, not in return.

SLIDE 3 — DEFINITION
QSL
Risk management

Volatility

  • Volatility is the typical size of the moves in an investment’s value over a chosen period.
  • The formal measure is the standard deviation of the returns for that period.
  • Read the formula as a sentence: how far a typical return sits from the average return.
  • The next slide applies it to AAPL and TSLA.
σ = √( Σ (ri − r̄)² ⁄ (N − 1) )
ri — each period’s return, for example one day’s percentage change
r̄ — the average of those returns
N — how many returns the period contains
σ — the volatility: one number for the whole period

SLIDE 4 — WORKED EXAMPLE
QSL
Risk management

Calculating volatility for AAPL and TSLA

  • Take each trading day’s percentage change in value. The bars show all twenty days for each stock, on the same scale.
  • Find the average daily return, and each day’s distance from that average.
  • The standard deviation of those daily returns is the daily volatility. For May 2026: AAPL 0.94%, TSLA 2.49%.
  • TSLA’s volatility was more than two and a half times AAPL’s. With one number per investment, the risk of any two investments can be compared directly.

SLIDE 5 — CONSIDERATIONS
QSL
Risk management

What volatility does and does not tell you

  • Volatility counts moves in both directions. A sharp rise raises it as much as a sharp fall. It measures the size of moves, not falling prices.
  • It carries the time scale of its returns. Daily returns give daily volatility. The convention for comparing investments is the annualized figure: the daily number scaled by the square root of 252 trading days, a factor of about sixteen.
  • It describes the period it was measured on. May’s volatility is a fact about May. Volatility changes over time, and calm months can be followed by rough ones.
  • One number cannot show the shape of the moves. Steady daily swings and rare large drops can produce the same volatility. Later units measure that difference.
